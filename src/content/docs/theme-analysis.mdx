Theme Analysis scans your data to identify recurring topics, ideas, and patterns. QInsights groups related content into themes, generates a title for each, and provides a short description supported by references back to your data.

Supported formats:

- **Word files** — typically interview or focus group transcripts
- **PDF files** — often articles and reports
- **Excel files** — ideal for open-ended survey responses, discussion boards, stakeholder feedback, or social media comments

## A content-mapping approach, not coding

Theme Analysis gives you a **high-level content map** of your data — a starting point that highlights areas worth exploring further, not a finished thematic analysis. While the term "theme" may evoke thematic analysis approaches like Braun and Clarke's, in QInsights themes are data-driven insights that surface early to help you navigate your material. They're exploratory and pragmatic, designed to give you an actionable entry point while you stay in control of the direction and depth of the analysis.

## How it works

QInsights analyzes your selected documents and identifies **2 to 10 themes**, each representing a significant topic or pattern, with a short description summarizing its relevance.

### Reviewing a theme (Word and PDF)

Click **Show more Details** on a theme to see its prevalence — how many documents it occurs in — and how many quotes support it.

<Screenshot label="Theme details" caption="Theme prevalence and description, with a link to export the theme with references." />

Click any document name under **References** to open a preview highlighting the supporting data segments in yellow. Use **Next**/**Previous** to move through them.

<Screenshot label="Validate in source" caption="Validating a theme against highlighted segments in the original transcript." />

> Highlights point you to the *right region* of the document, not to precisely coded segments — sometimes only half a sentence is highlighted. Treat them as pointers for validating Q's answer against your data, not as formal coding.

### Exporting results (Word and PDF)

Export your results as a PDF: the first section lists all themes with their prevalence (occurs / does not occur in X% of documents); the second lists each theme's description and per-document references. To get a Word file, click **Download**, then copy the preview into a Word document.

### References and export (Excel)

Click **Show More Details** on a theme to generate a detailed reference export: QInsights fetches every matching data segment (this can take a few minutes), and you can review the results — including all imported variables — before exporting.

<Screenshot label="Excel references" caption="Reviewing generated theme references, with imported spreadsheet variables shown alongside each quote." />

Click **Download** to export a single theme, or **Download all references** to export every theme at once.

## Exploring further with follow-up chats

Themes are a starting point, not an endpoint. Click the Q icon at the bottom of the themes view to open a follow-up chat linked to that Theme Analysis in the [Analysis Archive](/docs/analysis-archive). Use it to:

- prioritize which topics to investigate further, based on prevalence
- ask targeted questions about a specific theme
- get a fast sense of what your data is "about" before deeper analysis

<Screenshot label="Follow-up chat" caption="Asking a follow-up question about a specific theme, split-view alongside the theme cards." />

Click the arrow icon to expand the chat to full-screen, or again to return to the split view showing both the chat and the themes.

## Next steps

Once you've mapped the main themes, dig deeper with [Guided Conversational Analysis](/docs/guided-analysis) on a specific topic, or move to open-ended [Conversational Analysis](/docs/conversational-analysis).
